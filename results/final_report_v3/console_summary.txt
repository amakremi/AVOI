CONCLUSIONS
====================================================================================================
1. Stage 1 established: AVOI cut evidence acquisitions sharply on all three datasets, but did not beat information
   gain on unsafe assurance, lost to all-evidence on IAM expected loss, and exposed poor cross-testbed AIT model
   transfer. (Unchanged.)
2. Stage 2 established: a frozen evidence-model-adequacy gate makes only 4/9 evidence models (all IAM) eligible for
   autonomous use; on those, AVOI's acquisition-efficiency result replicates and the gate blocks autonomy wherever
   adequacy fails, at the cost of coverage. (Unchanged.)
3. Stage 3 newly established: (a) AVOI's next-evidence selection is genuinely history-dependent, not an artefact of a
   fixed ranking (A1 supported); (b) that adaptivity's benefit over a frozen acquisition order is real but
   objective-specific and can reverse when the evidence model is inadequate, not a general property (A2 not supported
   at the pre-registered pooled level, supported on 1 of 3 objectives); (c) the Stage-2 AIT-EXFIL failure was a
   fixable non-monotone-calibrator defect, and the same corrected methodology produces the project's first VALIDATED
   non-IAM evidence model, CERT-INSIDER (E1 supported); (d) even a VALIDATED external model can have zero
   operating-point sensitivity to the class that matters, and AVOI's efficiency advantage on it holds against some
   but not all baselines (E2 partially supported).
4. AVOI must not claim: that adaptive re-ranking is unconditionally better than a fixed order, that it generalizes to
   AIT-LDS v2.0, that CERT-style evidence models beyond the single validated claim (on a reduced r4.1+r5.1 release
   pool) are reliable, that a VALIDATED external model has good minority-class recall, or that Stage 3 was part of
   the original Stage-1/2 pre-registration -- it is a separately designed and frozen third stage.
