CONCLUSIONS
====================================================================================================
1. Stage 1 established: AVOI cut evidence acquisitions sharply on all three datasets, but did not beat information gain on unsafe assurance (H2 not supported), lost to all-evidence on IAM expected loss, H1 was not supported as registered, and AIT exposed poor cross-testbed model transfer.
2. Stage 2 newly established: with a frozen adequacy policy, only 4/9 evidence models (all IAM) are eligible; AIT and CERT models are inverted, unsupported or miscalibrated; on validated IAM models the acquisition-efficiency result replicates on new scenarios (S3), the gate blocks all autonomy where adequacy fails (S2) and every UAR reduction it causes costs coverage (S4); the prospective matched-safety construct was still degenerate.
3. AVOI can legitimately claim: substantially fewer acquisitions than exhaustive/fixed-order collection within adequacy-validated evidence models (via value-based stopping), lower unsafe assurance than symmetric generic VOI, an asymmetric-loss effect on unsafe assurance, and a governed abstention mechanism that withholds autonomy when model adequacy fails.
4. AVOI must not claim: lower UAR than information gain, lower loss than all-evidence, superiority over all strong baselines, efficiency at matched safety, generalisation to AIT-LDS/CERT, that the gate improves acquisition, pre-registration of the adequacy condition, regulatory compliance, or replacement of human assurance review.
